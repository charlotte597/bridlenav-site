Getting your ride out of whatever you track with.

The file is the only fiddly part of sending a route in, and it is fiddly for a daft reason: most tracking apps are far better at letting you look at your rides than at letting you have them. Here is where yours lives.

The file is called a GPX. It is just the line your ride drew, and it is the one thing we cannot work out for ourselves.

On your phone

These give you the file without a computer

OsmAnd

Records straight to a file on the phone, so there is nothing to export. Free.

Footpath

Open the ride and look for Share or Export, then choose GPX.

OS Maps

Open the route and look for Share or Export, then GPX.

Needs a computer, or paying

These two are the ones riders get stuck on

Strava

Record on the phone as usual, then open the ride on the Strava website, on a computer, and export the GPX from there. The phone app hides it. Annoying, and nothing to do with us.

Equilab

It is on the ride’s Summary page — but getting the file out needs their paid version. Your ride, behind a paywall. Worth knowing before you go looking.

Garmin

In Garmin Connect, open the activity and choose Export to GPX.

If you have never tracked a ride

Then don’t start by tracking one.

Plenty of the best rides anybody knows have never been recorded, because the person who knows them has been riding them for twenty years and never needed a map.

So tell us about it instead. Where you park, which way you set off, the lanes and landmarks you pass, where you turn, roughly how long it takes. We will plot the line from that and send the whole thing back to you to check. Nothing goes live until you have said it is right, and it goes on with your name on it.

A route put together that way is marked as documented rather than ridden, so nobody is misled about who has actually been.
