What makes one worth sharing

Four things, and only one of them is fiddly

Anyone may ride it

Public bridleway, byway, road or a path open to everyone. Not a field a farmer lets you cross, because that permission doesn’t travel to the strangers who will ride it on your word. This is the one thing we cannot check for you, and the one thing we ask you to confirm.

Parking a stranger can find

Where you actually leave the lorry, what the surface is like, and whether a trailer will turn. A postcode often drops somebody at the wrong end of a long lane, so we ask for the what3words square instead.

The words you’d say at each turn

This is the fiddly one, and it is also the whole point. BridleNav reads your words out loud as the rider approaches the junction, so “left through the gate by the barn” is worth far more than “turn left”. Gates, water and livestock get marked the same way.

Two photographs

A view, the going underfoot, the parking. They are what makes another rider pick your route, and they are shrunk in your browser before they are sent, which strips the location tag your camera put in them.

How it works

You probably don’t need to ride anything again

Most riders who track their hacks already have the hard part sitting in an app.

Send the file you already have

A GPX file out of Equilab, Strava, OS Maps, Footpath or a Garmin. In Equilab it’s on a ride’s Summary page; in Garmin Connect it’s Export to GPX. If you’ve never tracked a ride, record your next one in BridleNav instead and share it from the app when you’re home.

Trim it, then tell it what to say

Drag the start forward so the route doesn’t begin at your own yard — what you cut off never leaves the page. Then we show you where your line turns, and you write what you’d want said out loud at each one.

We read it before anybody rides it

Every route is read through by a person before it goes anywhere near the app. Nothing is published automatically, and if something doesn’t look right we come back to you rather than guess.

Your name on it

Routes are credited to the rider who found them

Every shared route carries a badge saying it came from a rider, with your name on it — or your horse’s name alongside yours, if that’s how you’d rather be known. If you’d sooner not be named at all, leave the name blank and the route goes up anonymously. Nobody has to put themselves on the internet to be useful.
